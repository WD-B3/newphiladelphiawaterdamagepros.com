Wet carpet gets heavy fast, and the pad beneath holds water like a sponge. Carpet water damage restoration in New Philadelphia, OH is about pulling out the water, drying the carpet, pad and subfloor, and deciding what can stay. The line is open 24/7 because the first day matters most.

Lift furniture off the carpet and put foil or plastic under the legs to stop stains. Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet. Water in a vacuum is a shock hazard.

How carpet reacts to water

Carpet fibers dry fairly well. The padding and the backing do not. Foam pad can hold a lot of water and can grow mold if it stays damp.

After about two days of being wet, the risk of odor and mold grows quickly. That is why timing matters more than the amount of water.

The water category decides everything. Clean water from a supply line can often be extracted and dried in place. Contaminated water, such as sewage, means carpet and pad are removed. See our sewage backup cleanup page for that case.

Save the carpet or replace it

Often saved

  • Clean water caught within a day or two.
  • Carpet with a good backing.
  • Pad that is replaced while carpet is dried.

Often replaced

  • Gray or black water of any kind.
  • Carpet wet for several days with odor.
  • Glued down carpet with a wet slab beneath.

Often the best result is to dry the carpet and replace the pad. The pad is cheap compared with the carpet, and removing it speeds drying of the floor underneath.

How we dry carpet

Extract

Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove most of the water.

Lift and float

The carpet is lifted from the tack strip and floated on air so the pad and floor can dry.

Remove the pad

Wet pad is cut out and replaced after drying.

Dry and sanitize

Air movers and dehumidifiers run with readings, and the carpet is sanitized if needed.

The subfloor below matters too. Wet wood or concrete under carpet keeps feeding moisture upward. Our structural drying page explains how we confirm it is dry. For rooms below grade, see our basement water damage cleanup page.

Common causes

  • Appliance and supply line leaks, such as a washer hose or a water heater leak.
  • Overflowing toilets and tubs.
  • Roof or ceiling leaks from above.
  • Burst pipes in winter, which are covered on our burst pipe water damage page.

Odor, stains and what comes after

A damp smell is the first sign that something stayed wet. Odor in carpet usually comes from the pad and the floor beneath it, not the carpet face. Deodorizing the surface rarely helps if the layers below are still damp. We check the readings in the pad area and the subfloor before we say a room is done.

Stains from brown tannins, dye or dirty water can show up after drying as the carpet wicks moisture to the surface. Cleaning after the drying is complete often improves them. If stains remain, a flooring contractor can tell you whether patching or replacement makes sense. Keep a sample piece from a closet, if there is one, for matching.

Whether carpet is dried in place or removed follows the water categories defined in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Carpet water damage FAQ

How long can wet carpet sit before it is ruined?

Clean water caught in the first day or two can usually be dried. After that, odor and mold risk rises quickly.

Can I dry carpet with fans and a dehumidifier myself?

For a small spill, maybe. For a soaked room, the pad and floor underneath are the problem, and household equipment is rarely enough.

Will the carpet shrink or stain?

Extraction and quick drying reduce the risk. Dyes in some carpets can bleed, and we tell you what we see.

Is carpet cleaning the same thing?

No. Cleaning treats the surface. Water damage restoration removes water from the pad and floor and confirms it with readings.

Does insurance pay for new carpet?

Sometimes, depending on the cause and your policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer.
