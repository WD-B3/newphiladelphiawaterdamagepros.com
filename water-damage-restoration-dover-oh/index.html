When water is spreading in Dover: what to do before we arrive

If water is moving across a floor, coming through a ceiling or rising in a basement in Dover, treat it as an emergency and call. Our dispatch line is open around the clock, every day, and we take Dover calls on nights, weekends and holidays. We offer same-day response when available.

Tell the dispatcher your street, what the water touched and whether you can reach the shutoff valve. Those three facts let us bring the right pumps and drying equipment on the first trip.

Stop the source

Close the main shutoff or the valve at the fixture, but only if you can reach it safely.

Cut power to wet rooms

Turn off breakers for wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.

Lift and photograph

Move small valuables and rugs up off the floor, and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

Keep people out

Keep children and pets out of standing water, especially if it came from a drain or toilet.

Why Dover homes take on water

Dover sits in a river valley, and its history is tied to flooding. The March 1913 flood hit the Dover area hard, and the later flood control work along the Tuscarawas and its tributaries changed how the valley handles heavy rain. Control structures help, but they do not stop a clogged gutter, a failed sump pump or a burst line. Local rain still finds low spots and basements.

Pull up your parcel on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, then ask Dover's building office what drainage rules apply to your lot. We do not quote flood zone numbers for neighborhoods, because flood zones follow individual parcels.

Basements and older homes

Many homes in Dover are older, and many sit on full basements. Older supply lines, aging water heaters and old cast iron drains are common sources of water problems. A basement with a sump pump needs that pump to work during a storm, so test it before the rainy season.

The Dover seasons and what they do to a house

Spring rain is the biggest basement risk. Heavy storms in the Tuscarawas Valley overload gutters and drains in a short time. Winter brings frozen pipes to garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls.

Summer humidity makes drying harder, so equipment runs longer. Fall leaves clog gutters and downspouts, which then spill water against foundations.

The cost of a water loss grows with each day water sits. If you can act in the first day, you save materials and shorten drying. The process we follow is on our structural drying and dehumidification page.
