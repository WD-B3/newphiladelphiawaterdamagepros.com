Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps in New Philadelphia, OH

A water damage claim goes better when the first day is organized. Here is the order that keeps you safe, protects the property and keeps the paper trail clean.

By the New Philadelphia Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026.

Filing a water damage claim is mostly paperwork, but the first hours are physical. Stop the water, make the building safe and record what happened. Everything else builds on those three things.

This article is general information, not insurance advice. Your policy and your insurer's process control what happens next.

Step 1: Make everyone safe

Before anything else, confirm that nobody is in danger from electricity, gas or contaminated water. If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911. If the water is sewage or river water, keep people and pets away from it.

Shutting off the main water valve, if you can reach it, stops many losses at the source. If you do not know where your valve is, our guide to finding it covers the common places.

Step 2: Stop more damage

Policies expect you to protect the property from further damage. That can mean extracting standing water, moving furniture, putting up temporary covers and starting drying. A restoration company does this work, and the work is usually part of the covered loss when the cause is covered.

Avoid starting repairs that would hide the damage, like new drywall or paint, before the insurer has seen it or before you have photographed it.

Step 3: Document everything

Photos and video are your best evidence. Take wide shots of each room and close shots of the source, the wet materials and any damaged belongings. Note the date and time, and write down what you noticed first.

Step 4: Call your insurer

Report the loss soon after it happens. Have your policy number, a short description of the cause and the date you found it. Ask what the next step is and whether an adjuster will visit, and write down the claim number and the name of the person you spoke with.

Ask the adjuster what the insurer wants you to keep and what you may discard. If wet items are hazardous, such as contaminated carpet, it is usually fine to remove them once they are photographed, but ask first when in doubt.

Step 5: Understand the scope

An adjuster writes an estimate of the covered work, and a contractor writes a scope and price for the actual work. They may differ. Share our scope and price with your adjuster if useful, and ask for an explanation if the two do not match.

We explain scope and price before work starts. Our structural drying and dehumidification page describes the monitoring that supports the drying part of a claim.

Step 6: Keep a log

Keep a simple log of calls, emails and visits with dates and names. Keep receipts for hotel stays and food if you cannot use your home, since some policies reimburse additional living expenses. Keep every document even after the claim closes.

If the claim is denied or reduced

Ask for the reason in writing. Compare it with your policy language, and ask for a second review if something looks wrong. The Ohio Department of Insurance accepts consumer complaints.

A public adjuster or attorney can help in larger or disputed claims. We document the work, but we do not argue claims.

Flood claims are separate

Flood claims, if you have flood insurance, are separate from homeowners claims and follow their own process. If river water entered your home, tell your agent immediately. Our flood damage cleanup page covers the physical cleanup.

Working with the adjuster

Adjusters visit to see the damage and estimate covered work. Be there if you can, and walk through each room with them. Point out the source, the wet areas and any hidden damage you know about, and hand over copies of your photos, not originals.

Ask how they calculate the contents portion of the claim, and whether the policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value. Replacement cost pays to buy a new item, while actual cash value subtracts depreciation. The difference can be large for older furniture and flooring.

If the adjuster's estimate leaves out work you believe is needed, send a written note with the reasons, and attach the moisture log or the restoration scope. Calm, specific paperwork works better than phone arguments.

According to the Ohio Department of Insurance, consumers can file complaints about claim handling, and standards for fair claim practices apply to insurers in the state.

What local records can support a claim

Tuscarawas County keeps a record of its major floods, including the March 1913 flood that devastated Dover and the surrounding area. If a claim turns on whether your location floods, the FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the official flood zone for your address.

Local weather records from the National Weather Service can confirm rain or wind on a given date, which can help when a storm caused a roof leak.

Claim steps FAQ

How soon do I have to report a water damage claim?

Policies require prompt notice. Report it as soon as you can. Check your policy for the exact language.

Can I start cleanup before the adjuster arrives?

Yes, and you should start emergency steps. Document first, and keep damaged materials unless the insurer says otherwise.

Does the restoration company talk to my insurer?

We can share photos, moisture logs and our scope with your insurer at your request. Coverage decisions remain with the insurer.
