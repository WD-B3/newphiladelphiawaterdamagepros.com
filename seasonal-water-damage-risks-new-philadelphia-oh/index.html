Seasonal Water Damage Risks in New Philadelphia, OH

Water finds a different way into the house in every season. Winter splits pipes, spring floods basements, summer drips from air conditioners and fall clogs gutters.

Northeast Ohio has four real seasons, and each brings a typical kind of water problem. Knowing the pattern helps you check the right things at the right time. The causes repeat every year.

This article is a seasonal calendar. It is general guidance and not a substitute for a professional inspection.

Winter: frozen and split pipes

Cold snaps freeze water in pipes in garages, crawl spaces, exterior walls and unheated rooms. The ice itself rarely breaks the pipe at the frozen spot. Pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet, and the pipe splits. The leak starts when the ice melts.

Keep the heat on, even if you are away, open cabinet doors on exterior walls during hard cold and know where your main shutoff is. Disconnect outdoor hoses before the first freeze. Our frozen pipe water damage page has the full list.

Spring: thaw, rain and basements

Spring brings snow melt and heavy rain on soil that may still be frozen or saturated. Water has nowhere to go, so it finds basements, window wells and crawl spaces. Sump pumps work hardest now.

Ice dams, which form when melt water refreezes at the roof edge, can push water under shingles, and the stains show up on ceilings in early spring. See ceiling water damage repair and basement water damage cleanup.

Summer: humidity, AC and storms

Air conditioners make condensation, and their drain lines clog with algae. An overflowing drain pan can soak a ceiling or a closet floor. Thunderstorms can drop a lot of rain in a short time and overwhelm drains.

High humidity slows drying and encourages musty smells, especially in basements. Run a dehumidifier and check that your AC drain is clear. Our AC leak water damage page covers the drain lines.

Fall: leaves, gutters and heating season

Leaves clog gutters and downspouts, and then fall rain overflows them against the foundation. This is the best time to clean gutters, check the roof and insulate pipes before freezing weather.

Water heaters work harder when the weather cools, and an old tank may fail. Check your tank for rust at the base, mineral crust on fittings and moisture on the floor. See water heater leak cleanup.

Year-round risks

Some failures do not follow the calendar. Washing machine hoses, dishwasher lines and toilet supply lines can fail at any time. Replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel lines about every five years, and check under sinks twice a year.

Know where your main shutoff is. A burst line releases a lot of water quickly, and shutting the valve is the single most useful thing you can do.

A yearly checklist you can actually finish

Pick two weekends, one in spring and one in fall. In spring, test the sump pump, check the basement for new stains, look under sinks, inspect the roof from the ground and run the air conditioner for a few minutes to see that the drain flows.

In fall, clean the gutters, look at the water heater, disconnect and drain outdoor hoses, insulate exposed pipes in garages and crawl spaces and check the weather stripping on basement doors and windows. Test your smoke and carbon monoxide detectors at the same time.

Write the dates down and keep the list. A short checklist that gets done beats a long one that gets ignored.

Matching your budget to the season

Because the risks repeat, you can plan for them. Set aside a small amount each year for gutter cleaning, hose replacement and a plumber's checkup of the water heater and shutoffs. These items cost a fraction of a single water damage repair.

Keep an emergency kit with a pipe wrap, a few towels, a flashlight, work gloves and the phone numbers you would need. Tape the number for your plumber and for us inside a cabinet door.

If your home is older, ask a plumber about the age and material of your supply lines. Some older materials fail more often than newer ones, and replacing them before they fail is cheaper than the flood.

Guidelines from FEMA and the National Weather Service both recommend preparing before each season instead of during it.

Weather in the Tuscarawas Valley

The valley around New Philadelphia sees a full range of weather, from hard winter freezes to humid summers and heavy storms. The March 1913 flood that hit Dover and the county was a spring event, a reminder that spring is the season when rain, thaw and rivers combine.

The flood control system tied to the Muskingum Watershed Conservancy District helps with river flooding, but it does not stop frozen pipes or roof leaks.

Seasonal risk FAQ

Which season has the most water damage calls?

Winter and spring are busy because of frozen pipes and basement water, but leaks happen year round.

When should I replace washing machine hoses?

About every five years, or sooner if you see cracks or bulges.

Does the season change how long drying takes?

Yes. Humid summer weather slows drying, while dry winter air can speed it up.
