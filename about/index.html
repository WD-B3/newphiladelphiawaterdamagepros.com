What we do

We remove standing water, take out materials that cannot dry, dry what can be saved and confirm the result with moisture readings. We follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which is the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. S500 is a standard, not a certification we claim. You can see every service on our services page.

What we do not do

We do not repair roofs or fix plumbing. We do not waterproof foundations or rebuild drywall, floors and paint. Those are separate trades, and we work alongside them.

We also do not promise insurance outcomes. We document the loss, and your insurer makes the claim decision.

Local, because the valley is local

Water problems here follow the Tuscarawas River valley, old houses with full basements and a climate with real winters. That is why our pages talk about basement water damage, frozen pipes and storm water in plain terms. Our service area page lists the towns we cover.

What a typical job looks like

Most calls start the same way. A dispatcher takes your address, asks where the water is coming from and tells you what is safe to do while you wait. A technician arrives with extraction equipment and moisture meters, finds the source of the water if it is still running and maps how far it has spread.

After the assessment, we explain the plan in plain words. That includes what gets pumped, what gets removed, what equipment goes where and how many days of drying we expect. You hear the price before work starts, and you can ask questions until it makes sense.

Drying then runs for a few days in most homes. A technician returns to take readings, adjust equipment and tell you what the numbers say. When readings reach dry standards, the equipment comes out and you get a summary of what was done.

Equipment and measurements

Restoration is a measuring job. We use moisture meters to read drywall, wood and concrete, and thermal imaging to find cool, wet areas behind finished surfaces. Air movers push dry air across wet materials, and dehumidifiers pull the water out of the air so it does not settle back in.

Each piece of equipment has a purpose, and we explain it. Our structural drying and dehumidification page walks through the method for homeowners who want the details.

What we ask of customers

We ask a few things that make the job go better. Tell us everything you know about how the water started, including when and what you did. Keep photos of the damage before cleanup. Keep damaged items until your insurer says they can go, unless they are a safety risk.

We also ask that you call early. The first day matters more than any other day in a water loss, and a phone call costs nothing. Whether the cleanup turns out to be big or small, it is easier to stop a small problem than to repair a large one.

Why we are plain about limits

Water damage work attracts big promises, and we try to avoid them. We do not promise a particular arrival minute, because traffic, weather and the number of calls change it. We offer same-day response when available and tell you honestly when it is not.

We also do not promise what your insurance will pay. We document the loss with photos and moisture logs so you have solid records to share, and your insurer makes the coverage decision. If something is outside our work, such as a roof repair or a plumbing fix, we say so early and point you to the right trade.
