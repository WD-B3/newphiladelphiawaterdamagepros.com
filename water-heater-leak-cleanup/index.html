A leaking water heater is one of the most common household water losses, and it can be sudden or slow. Emergency water heater leak cleanup in New Philadelphia, OH covers the water on the floor and the moisture behind it. The line is open 24/7, because tanks tend to give out overnight when the house is quiet.

Turn off the electric breaker or the gas valve to the heater, close the cold water supply valve on top of the tank and keep everyone away from the puddle if the heater is near outlets. Then call.

When a water heater leak is an emergency

A heater that is gushing from the bottom or from a split seam is an emergency, and so is any heater with water around a gas appliance or electric panel. A small drip from the pressure relief valve or a fitting is less urgent, but it is still a leak that will damage the floor if left alone.

Tell the dispatcher what kind of heater it is, where it sits and whether water has reached carpet, a finished room or a floor drain. Heaters in basements may flood stored boxes, and heaters in upstairs closets can leak through to the ceiling below.

Gas and electric first

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility. If the heater is electric and water is around it, do not touch the unit. Turn off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Why tanks fail

Tank heaters corrode from the inside, and the steel liner eventually pits through. Sediment buildup and hard water speed that up. Connections, the temperature and pressure relief valve and the drain valve can also leak without the tank itself failing.

A heater that is old, rumbling or leaving rust-colored water at the tap is telling you something. A plumber can say whether the leak is a fitting you can repair or a tank that has to be replaced. That decision is separate from the cleanup.

Signs of a slow leak

  • A damp or rusted pan under the tank.
  • Rust streaks at the base or on the fittings.
  • Warped flooring or a soft spot near the heater.
  • A musty smell in a utility room or closet.
  • A rising water bill with no clear cause.

What the cleanup involves

Stop the water

Close the supply and kill the power or gas so the leak is contained.

Extract

Pump or vacuum the standing water from the floor and low spaces.

Open and inspect

Check the walls, subfloor and anything stored nearby with moisture meters.

Dry and verify

Equipment runs with readings each day, and it stays until the floor and walls reach target.

Most heater leaks involve clean supply water, which is easier to deal with than contaminated water. Materials such as drywall behind the heater and flooring in the closet still need to be checked, so see wall and drywall water damage repair and structural drying for how those stages work. Water heater leak FAQ

Is a leaking water heater something I should call about after hours?

Yes. A tank that is leaking can empty onto the floor, so call any time. Shut off the cold feed and the power or gas first if it is safe.

Should I drain the tank myself?

If the leak is serious, shutting off the supply and power is the safest first step. A plumber can drain and replace the tank.

Do you replace water heaters?

No. A licensed plumber handles the heater. We handle the water, the drying and the documentation.

How much water can a tank release?

A typical residential tank holds roughly 40 to 50 gallons, and all of it can come out if the tank fails.

Will insurance pay for the cleanup?

Sudden failures are often covered, while gradual corrosion leaks may not be. Whether this is covered turns on your policy wording, which only your insurer can interpret.
