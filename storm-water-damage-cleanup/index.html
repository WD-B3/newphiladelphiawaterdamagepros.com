Storm water damage starts with weather and ends in your house. Emergency storm water damage cleanup in New Philadelphia, OH covers water that came in through a damaged roof, a failed window, a wall or a flooded basement after a heavy rain. The 24/7 line is open during and after a storm, when many homes have water problems at the same time.

If the storm is still active, stay inside and away from windows. After it passes, avoid downed power lines, stay out of flooded rooms with live electricity and photograph the damage before moving anything.

When storm water is an emergency

Active water from a damaged roof, a broken window or a basement taking on rain is an emergency. Water in contact with outlets, panels or appliances is a safety issue before it is a restoration job. So is a ceiling that is bulging.

Tell the dispatcher where the water entered, whether the storm is still going and whether any structural damage, such as a fallen tree, has occurred. Call your insurer too, but do not wait on that to protect the property.

After the storm

Downed power lines can be live. Do not touch anything in contact with a line, and call the utility or 911. If a tree is on the roof or the house shifted, leave and call 911 before you call us.

How storm water gets in

Roof and flashing damage

Wind lifts shingles and rain reaches decking, insulation and the ceiling below.

Windows and doors

Wind-driven rain pushes past seals, especially around older frames.

Gutters and downspouts

Clogged gutters overflow against the siding and foundation.

Surface runoff

Heavy rain flows toward low points and finds basements and window wells.

Water that rises from outside and floods a property is a separate scenario, covered on our flood damage cleanup page. A plain roof leak with no storm is more of a ceiling water damage repair issue.

What storms look like in Tuscarawas County

The county sits in the Tuscarawas River valley, and its history shows how much water can come through. The March 1913 flood devastated Dover and the surrounding county and led to a flood control system managed by the Muskingum Watershed Conservancy District. That system helps, but it does not make a roof leak or a flooded window well go away.

We do not quote storm dates or rainfall totals here. To see how your own address is mapped, use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask the city about local drainage rules.

What we do after a storm

Assess

Walk the property and find where water entered.

Extract

Remove standing water from floors and low rooms.

Protect

Recommend temporary roof or window covers from a contractor where needed.

Dry

Run air movers and dehumidifiers with daily moisture readings.

We do not repair roofs or windows. A roofer or contractor does that. Our work is the water, the drying and the documentation. Details of the drying stage are on our structural drying page, and basement water is covered on our basement water damage cleanup page.

Documenting storm damage

Photograph the exterior and the interior, with wide shots and close-ups. Note the date and time. Keep receipts for temporary repairs. These notes help your insurer, and we also keep our own moisture readings and photos.

Storm water damage FAQ

Do you work during and right after a storm?

Yes. The line stays open through storms. Demand rises after a big storm, so tell us the damage and we set priorities with you.

Should I tarp my roof myself?

Do not climb on a wet roof. A roofer or contractor can place a temporary cover. We handle the inside.

Is storm damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Wind and rain damage often are, while flood is usually excluded. Your insurer can tell you what your policy pays for in this situation.

How do I document the loss?

Photograph and video the damage, note the date and keep receipts. Do not throw away damaged items until your insurer says so.
