Water Damage in a Rental in New Philadelphia, OH: What Tenants and Landlords Should Do

When a rental floods or leaks, the first problem is the water and the second is who handles it. Here is a clear order of steps for both sides.

A water problem in a rental involves three parties: the tenant, the landlord and, often, an insurer. The first move is the same for everyone. Stop the water if it is safe, protect people and belongings and tell the landlord in writing.

This article is general information and not legal advice. A lease and Ohio law decide the details. A legal aid office or attorney can help with disputes.

What tenants should do first

Tell the landlord or property manager right away, by phone and then in writing so there is a record. Take photos and video of the water and the damage. Move belongings out of harm's way and keep receipts for anything you spend.

If water is spreading and nobody answers, call an emergency line. You can call us for extraction, but the landlord must authorize repairs to the unit, so keep the landlord informed. If there is an electrical or gas risk, call the utility or 911.

What landlords should do first

Stop the source, usually by shutting off water at the valve or main, and arrange extraction and drying quickly. Delay turns a repair into a larger loss and a larger dispute. Notify the building insurer and document the loss with photos.

Landlords should give tenants clear information about access, timing and temporary housing if the unit cannot be used.

What Ohio law says

Ohio Revised Code section 5321.04 sets landlord duties, including keeping the premises in a fit and habitable condition and keeping plumbing in good working order. If a landlord does not fix a problem after written notice, section 5321.07 lays out a process where the tenant may deposit rent with the clerk of court. A tenant should not simply stop paying rent without following the law.

Because disputes can be costly, talk to a legal aid office or an attorney before using that process.

Renters insurance matters

Your landlord's insurance covers the building, not your clothes, furniture and electronics. Renters insurance covers your belongings after covered events like a burst pipe, and it may help with extra living costs. If you rent and have no policy, consider getting one.

Keeping a paper trail

In a rental, a clear record protects both sides. Tenants should keep copies of every text, email and letter, with dates, and save photos and videos with their original timestamps. Landlords should record when they were notified, what they did and who they hired.

If you send a letter, send it in a way that proves delivery, and keep a copy. Describe the problem plainly, include the date it started and state what you want done. Avoid angry language, since the letter may later be read by an insurer, a lawyer or a court clerk.

Landlords should keep invoices, moisture logs and photos of the finished repairs. They help with insurance claims, with deposit disputes and with the next tenant.

Security deposits and move-out

Water damage can lead to disputes about deposits, so keep before and after photos. Tenants should document the condition of the unit at move-in and record any damage that was already there. Landlords who deduct for damage should be ready to show what happened and what it cost.

Under Ohio law, deposit deductions require an itemized list sent within thirty days after the tenant leaves and gives a forwarding address. Both sides should read the lease for any clauses about water, plumbing and notice, and keep copies of everything they sign.

If the damage was caused by a failure in the building, such as a roof leak or a burst pipe, it is generally not the tenant's fault. If it was caused by a tenant's actions, such as leaving a tub running, the cost may be assigned to the tenant under the lease.

What to do before help arrives

  • Close the shutoff valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Keep away from wet electrical outlets.
  • Lift belongings off the floor and photograph the damage.
  • Tell the landlord in writing.
  • Call the 24/7 line.

Rentals in the New Philadelphia area

New Philadelphia is a county seat and, with Dover, part of a twin cities area, so many rental units are in older houses divided into apartments and small multi-unit buildings. A leak in one unit often reaches the unit below it, which is why quick shutoff and documentation matter in both.

The Ohio Revised Code sections above apply across the state, including New Philadelphia, Dover and Zoar.

Rental water damage FAQ

Who pays for a leak in a rental?

It depends on the cause, the lease and the policies involved. The landlord usually handles building repairs, and the tenant's renters insurance usually handles belongings.

Can I withhold rent after a leak?

Not simply by stopping payment. Ohio has a specific process, so get legal advice first.

Should I move out?

If the unit is unsafe, talk to the landlord and consider your options. Keep records of any costs.
