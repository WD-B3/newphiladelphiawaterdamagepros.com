A basement is the lowest room in the house, and it is where water ends up. Emergency basement water damage cleanup in New Philadelphia, OH starts with removing the water and ends with a dry, safe space. The 24/7 line is open because basements flood during storms, thaws and power outages, which are not business-hours events.

Do not go into a basement with standing water if the panel, a heater or outlets are below the waterline. Call the electric utility or 911 for electrical risks, and then call us.

When a wet basement is an emergency

Water is rising, covers more than a few feet or has reached finished walls, carpet or stored items. Water near the furnace, the water heater or the electrical panel is a safety problem first. A sump pump that has stopped or is running nonstop is a warning, too.

Tell the dispatcher the depth, whether it is still rising, whether the sump pump has power and what is stored down there. If water came from a toilet or floor drain, tell us that immediately because it changes the safety plan.

Panels, furnaces and gas

If the water is touching a furnace, water heater, panel or outlets, do not enter. Call the electric utility or 911. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911.

Why basements take on water

Failed sump pump

A pump loses power, clogs or fails during heavy rain.

Foundation seepage

Water pushes through cracks, tie holes and the wall floor joint.

Window wells and grading

Poor drainage sends rain to the foundation.

Drains and sewer lines

A blocked drain or backed up sewer sends water up through the floor.

Restoration is not waterproofing. We dry and restore the space, and we do not fix drainage or foundation problems. A foundation contractor or waterproofing company does that. We can tell you what we saw.

Local ground conditions

New Philadelphia lies along the Tuscarawas River, and groundwater can rise with the river and heavy rain. Many homes in this part of Ohio have full basements, and the age of the house, the grading and the drainage all vary. We do not quote basement counts or groundwater depths. Ask the city building department about drainage requirements for your lot and check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Signs of basement water trouble

  • A damp smell or white mineral deposits on masonry.
  • Water stains at the base of walls.
  • Peeling paint and bubbling finishes on lower walls.
  • Warped paneling or swollen trim in a finished basement.
  • A sump pump that cycles constantly.

What we do

Make it safe

Confirm power and gas are safe and the source is stopped.

Pump and extract

Remove standing water with submersible pumps and extractors.

Remove what cannot dry

Wet carpet, pad, insulation and paneling are cut out and bagged.

Dry and verify

LGR dehumidifiers and air movers stay until masonry and framing read dry.

Finished basements need extra care because water hides behind paneling and under carpet. Carpet and pad are covered on our carpet water damage restoration page, and the monitoring is on our structural drying page. If the water was contaminated, see sewage backup cleanup. For river or groundwater flooding, read our flood damage cleanup page.

Stored belongings

Cardboard boxes, upholstery and papers do not recover well from basement water. Move them to dry ground and sort them. Photograph anything of value before it is thrown out, and keep the list for your insurer.

Basement water damage FAQ

Will you come out for basement water in the middle of the night?

Yes. Basements tend to flood in storms, so the line is open all night. Stay out of water that reaches outlets or the panel.

Do you waterproof basements?

No. We extract and dry. A foundation or waterproofing contractor fixes drainage and seepage.

Can I run a shop vacuum on basement water?

Only for a few inches of clean water and only with power safely away from the water. For anything more, call.

How long does a basement take to dry?

Often several days. Concrete holds water, and moisture readings decide when it is done.

Does homeowners insurance cover a flooded basement?

It depends on the cause. Check your policy or ask your insurer about flood and sewer backup coverage.
