The storm of April 29, 2025

On Tuesday, April 29, 2025, strong winds hit New Philadelphia in the afternoon. A news report described trees roughly 50 feet tall falling onto homes on 1st Drive NE, and a large limb crashing through the windows of a home on North Broadway. Thousands of customers lost power. Crews were cutting up limbs the next morning.

That event is the clearest storm record we found for this part of the city. It does not mean every home here has a leak. It does show how quickly a tree can turn into a water problem. A broken window or a torn roof lets the next rain straight into a living room or attic.

From a broken roof to a wet ceiling

After tree damage, water usually follows in two ways. Rain comes through the opening and runs down walls, or it soaks insulation in the attic and later shows as a brown stain on the ceiling. Wet insulation and drywall hold water long after the sky clears, so a ceiling that looks only a little stained can have a lot of wet material above it.

Call your insurer and a roofer or window contractor for the opening itself. Our work is the water inside, including extraction, removal of material that cannot dry and drying until readings are back to normal.

Safety after a tree falls

Stay away from downed power lines and anything touching them, and call 911 or the power provider. Do not enter a room with a sagging ceiling, since wet drywall can fall. If the roof is open and you cannot cover it safely, wait for a professional.

Do not turn on lights or appliances in a room where water has reached outlets. Photograph the tree, the opening and the wet areas for your insurer once it is safe. Keep children and pets out of wet rooms.

What to do before we arrive

Water from a storm is often wetter than it looks.

Watch for hazards

Stay out of rooms with downed lines, sagging ceilings or broken glass.

Cover the opening if safe

Use a tarp or plastic only from a stable position, and never climb a wet roof.

Move what you can

Lift small items and rugs off wet floors and take photos first.

Leave breakers alone in water

Do not touch a panel or switch while standing in water, and call the utility if needed.

What a storm job looks like

Call

A dispatcher takes your address and asks where the water is entering.

Assess

The crew checks the wet rooms, the attic access and the moisture in framing.

Remove

Standing water is extracted and wet insulation and drywall are cut out when they cannot dry.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run with daily readings until the structure is dry.

Our drying plan uses the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration as its guide. Photos of the damage, the fallen tree and daily moisture readings can help your insurer see the loss. Coverage depends on the policy and on the cause of the damage, and some policies treat wind damage and flood differently. Ask your agent. We do not decide coverage.
