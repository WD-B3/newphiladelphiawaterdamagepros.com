A frozen pipe is a slow problem that can become a fast one. Emergency frozen pipe water damage cleanup in New Philadelphia, OH usually starts at the thaw, when ice that was holding a crack closed melts and water pours through. Our 24/7 line answers during the cold nights when this tends to happen.

If water is flowing, shut off the main valve first. If a pipe is frozen but not leaking, keep the faucet open, warm the area gently with a space heater placed safely away from anything flammable and call if you cannot find the frozen section. Never use an open flame.

When a frozen pipe is an emergency

Call at once if you see water, hear running water in a wall, find a bulge or frost on a pipe or have no water at all during a deep cold snap. A pipe that has stopped flowing may already be split. The water you see on the floor after the thaw is the moment the damage begins, so treat a no-water morning as a warning.

Never use flame on a pipe

Torches and open flames on or near pipes are a fire risk, especially near insulation and framing. Use a hair dryer or warm towels only, and keep any heat source away from standing water.

What actually happens

Water expands when it freezes. The pressure usually builds between the ice blockage and a closed faucet, and that is where a pipe splits. The split can be tiny. Water only escapes when the ice melts, which may be hours or days after the pipe froze.

Common freeze points are pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawl spaces, unheated basements and under sinks against cold walls. Check the Ohio forecast and the age of your insulation. We do not quote local freeze statistics here because they vary by winter and by house.

Signs of a frozen or split pipe

  • Faucets that run a trickle or not at all in cold weather.
  • Frost or a bulge on an exposed pipe.
  • A damp spot or drip near a pipe that was fine yesterday.
  • A sudden stain on a ceiling or wall.
  • The sound of running water after a thaw.

What we do

The pipe repair belongs to a plumber. The water belongs to us. Once the source is stopped, crews extract standing water, locate hidden wet insulation and drywall with moisture meters and thermal imaging and dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers. If the split was inside a wall, a careful opening is usually cut so the cavity can dry, and we explain which sections need to come out.

Water that ran inside an exterior wall often wets insulation that holds moisture. The wall side of this problem is on our wall and drywall water damage repair page, and the general burst scenario is on our burst pipe water damage page.

Preventing the next freeze

Insulate exposed lines

Foam sleeves on pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls slow heat loss.

Let faucets drip

A slow drip on the coldest nights keeps water moving past the ice point.

Open cabinet doors

Warm room air reaches the pipes under sinks on exterior walls.

Know your shutoff

Find and label your main valve before winter, not during a leak.

Frozen pipe FAQ

Are frozen pipe calls taken on holidays and in the worst cold?

Yes. Cold snaps cause many calls at once, so we ask you to call early. The line stays open every day of the year.

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

A hair dryer or warm towels on a pipe with the faucet open can work for a pipe that has not split. Never use a flame, and call if you cannot find the frozen section.

Why did my pipe only leak after it warmed up?

Ice can plug a split. When the ice melts, the plug goes and water flows through the crack.

How do I know if water is inside my wall?

Look for stains, a musty smell, a cold wet spot or the sound of running water. A moisture meter and thermal camera can confirm it.

Does homeowners insurance cover frozen pipes?

Often for sudden damage, sometimes with conditions about heat in the home. Read the water damage and exclusions sections of your policy, or ask your insurer.
