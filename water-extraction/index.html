When there is standing water, the first job is to get it out. Emergency water extraction in New Philadelphia, OH means pumps, extraction wands and trained hands removing water from carpet, hard floors and low spaces before it soaks further. The line answers 24/7, so a flooded kitchen at 2 a.m. gets the same attention as one at noon.

While you wait, shut off the water source if you can do it safely, move electronics and valuables to dry surfaces and keep children and pets out of the room. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

When standing water is an emergency

Any water you can see pooled on a floor is already working its way sideways. It wicks up baseboards, runs under flooring and drops into the floor below if there is one. The first hours are the cheapest time to stop it.

Call right away when water covers more than a small area, when it has reached carpet, when it came from a toilet or drain or when it is near outlets. Say whether the source is stopped. If it is not, the dispatcher will walk you through the main shutoff.

Do not wade in if power is on

If outlets, power strips or appliances are in the water, do not step in. Switch off breakers only when the panel is dry and reachable from dry ground. Otherwise call 911 or the electric utility first.

Equipment that does the work

Extraction is not one machine. The tool depends on how much water there is and what it is sitting on.

Submersible pumps

Move large volumes from basements and low rooms, often several inches deep.

Truck-mounted and portable extractors

Pull water out of carpet, pad and hard floors using strong suction.

Weighted extraction wands

Squeeze water out of carpet and pad in place when they can still be saved.

Moisture meters and thermal cameras

Show where water went after the surface looks dry.

What happens after the water is out

Extraction removes the water you can see. It does not dry the building. Wet drywall, framing and subfloor hold moisture for days, and that is when odors and damage start. A crew follows extraction with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, which is covered on our structural drying and dehumidification page.

Hardwood and laminate are a separate decision. Some floors can be dried in place with special mats, and some cannot, as explained on our hardwood floor water damage repair page. Carpet that sat in clean water for a short time can often be saved, while carpet that soaked in contaminated water is replaced.

Where the water usually comes from

Water extraction calls in this area tend to start the same few ways. A supply line fails under a sink, a washing machine hose lets go, a water heater tank splits, a heavy storm pushes water through a basement wall or a toilet overflows. Each source changes the water category and the safety rules, so tell us the source on the call.

Homes near the Tuscarawas River and its creeks can also take water from the ground. That kind of water is treated differently from a clean supply line break, as covered on our flood damage cleanup page.

Signs water went further than you think

  • The wall base is damp when you press a finger to it.
  • Floorboards are cupping or the floor feels springy.
  • A ceiling below the wet room has a brown stain or sag.
  • Wallpaper or paint is bubbling.
  • A damp smell lingers after fans have run.

Extraction FAQ

Will someone answer if water is standing on my floor at 2 a.m.?

Yes. The line is staffed all night, every night. A crew heads out the same day when the schedule allows.

How much water is too much to handle myself?

A shallow spill from a clean source on a hard floor can be mopped. Anything that has soaked carpet, reached a wall or covers a large area should be extracted properly, because the water you cannot see is the expensive part.

Can carpet be saved after extraction?

Sometimes. Clean water that sat briefly gives carpet a fair chance, while contaminated water usually means replacement. A crew will tell you which case you have.

What is the difference between extraction and drying?

Extraction removes liquid water. Drying removes the moisture trapped in materials, which is a separate stage with its own equipment.
