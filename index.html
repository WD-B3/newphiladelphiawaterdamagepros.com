What to do in the first hour

The first hour decides how much of the building can be saved. These steps are safe for most homes. If anything seems unsafe, skip it and call.

Stop the source

Close the main shutoff or the fixture valve if you can reach it safely.

Make it safe

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry. Stay out of rooms with water near outlets.

Document

Photograph and video the damage and the source before moving things.

Call us

Call the 24/7 line and tell us what you see.

If you do not know where your main shutoff is, find it now, before you need it. A burst line can release a lot of water in a short time.

How restoration works

Restoration has four jobs: remove the water, remove what cannot dry, dry what can and confirm the result with measurements. Crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, an industry standard you can ask any company about.

Fast extraction

Standing water is pumped and extracted first, because every hour it sits it spreads into more material.

Measured drying

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water is hiding, and readings tell us when the job is done.

Clear scope

You get a written explanation of the work and the price before work starts, not after.

Documentation

Photos and moisture logs support your insurance conversation. We do not promise claim outcomes.

Water damage and insurance

Think about your policy early

Many homeowners policies cover sudden events such as a burst pipe or an appliance failure, and many exclude flood, groundwater and gradual seepage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph everything before cleanup begins. We document the work, and we do not promise a claim result.

Water problems we see in the Tuscarawas Valley

New Philadelphia sits on the Tuscarawas River, along Interstate 77, US 250 and State Routes 39 and 800. The river valley has a long history of flooding. The great flood of March 1913 hit the county hard and led to flood control work under the Muskingum Watershed Conservancy District. That work reduces risk, but it does not stop a failed sump pump, a split supply line or a roof leak.

Day to day, the calls are ordinary. Freezing weather splits pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls. Spring rain tests basements.

Older water heaters and washer hoses fail without warning. Our basement water damage cleanup and burst pipe water damage pages cover the most common causes.

Local places make good directions for a dispatcher. Tuscora Park, Schoenbrunn Village, Kent State University at Tuscarawas and New Towne Mall are all easy reference points when you give us your address. The Tuscarawas River also runs through the city, and low ground near it is where flooding shows up first. We do not quote flood zone numbers by street, so use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your parcel.

Questions about water damage restoration

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in New Philadelphia?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day, including holidays. We aim for same-day response and say so when we cannot.

What should I do first when water is spreading?

Stop the source if you can do it safely, stay away from electricity near water and photograph the damage. Then call (740) 231-9132.

How long does water damage take to dry?

Small clean water jobs often take a few days, while larger or contaminated jobs take longer. Moisture readings decide, not the calendar. See structural drying and dehumidification.

How much will it cost?

It depends on the water category, the area, the materials and the drying time. Scope and price come first, then the work. See the cost table above.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Sudden events such as a burst pipe are often covered, while flood and long-term seepage often are not. Coverage is set by your policy language, and your agent can read it with you.

New Philadelphia, Dover and Zoar, plus nearby places in Tuscarawas County. Confirm by phone.
