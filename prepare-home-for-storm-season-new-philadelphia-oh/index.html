How to Prepare Your Home for Storm Season in New Philadelphia, OH

Most storm water damage starts with something small and fixable, like a clogged gutter or a dead sump pump battery. A weekend of checks before the rains can prevent a week of cleanup.

Storms test a house where it is weakest. Water takes the easiest path, and that path is often a clogged gutter, a loose flashing or a basement window well. Checking those places before storm season costs far less than cleaning up after.

This is a homeowner checklist, not a contractor's inspection. If you are not comfortable on a ladder or on a roof, hire someone for those tasks.

Gutters and downspouts

Gutters carry roof water away from the foundation. When they clog, water spills over the edge and soaks the ground beside the house. Clean them of leaves and debris, and check that downspouts empty at least several feet from the foundation.

Look for sagging sections, loose seams and standing water in the gutter. Splash blocks or extensions help direct water away.

Roof and flashing

From the ground or with binoculars, look for missing, curled or cracked shingles. Check the flashing around chimneys, vents and skylights, since those are the most common leak points. Do not climb on a wet or steep roof.

If you have an attic, look at the underside of the roof after rain for stains, damp insulation and daylight. Our ceiling water damage repair page explains what roof leaks do inside the house.

Sump pump and basement

Test the pump

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump turns on and drains.

Check the discharge

The pipe should carry water well away from the house.

Test backup power

A battery backup helps if the power fails during a storm.

Clear window wells

Remove leaves and make sure wells drain.

Basements flood when the pump fails during the very storm it was meant for. Our basement water damage cleanup page explains what to do if it happens.

Grading and drainage

Soil next to the foundation should slope away from the house. Settled soil, flower beds that trap water and walkways that tilt toward the house all send water toward the foundation. Adding soil to restore slope is a common low cost fix.

Check for low spots in the yard where water ponds after rain, especially near the house. A contractor can regrade or add drainage if it is a bigger problem.

Floor drains and sewer backup

Floor drains can back up when the municipal system is overloaded. Keep drains clear and know where your cleanout is. If you have a history of backup, ask your insurer about sewer backup coverage and your plumber about a backwater valve.

Insurance and documents

Review your homeowners policy before storm season. Know your deductible and whether you have sewer backup and flood coverage. Take photos or video of each room and your belongings, and store a copy online.

When water gets in anyway

Stop the source if you can, stay away from electricity near water and photograph the damage. Call your insurer and a restoration company. Our storm water damage cleanup and flood damage cleanup pages describe what happens next.

Inside the house

Storm preparation is not only outdoor work. Know where your water main, electrical panel and gas shutoff are, and make sure everyone in the house does too. Keep a flashlight, a phone charger and a battery radio where you can find them without light.

Lift valuables off basement floors and store papers in sealed plastic bins instead of cardboard. Cardboard soaks up water and falls apart. Move electronics off the floor and away from exterior walls.

Take photos or a short video of every room before storm season starts. If you ever need to file a claim, a record of what you owned and what the room looked like is worth a great deal.

What to do during a storm

Stay inside and away from windows. If water starts to enter, move belongings up and away from it and keep out of any room where water is near outlets, appliances or the panel. Do not go into a flooded basement, even to check on the pump.

When the storm passes, look from a safe distance for downed lines, damaged roof sections and trees leaning on the house. Photograph everything before you move anything, and call your insurer and a restoration company about wet areas.

According to FEMA flood guidance, checking your flood zone and coverage before storm season is a basic preparation step, and local building departments set drainage standards for your lot.

A valley that has flooded before

The Tuscarawas River valley has flooded before, and the March 1913 flood hit Dover and the surrounding county hard. Flood control work under the Muskingum Watershed Conservancy District reduced the risk, but it does not stop local drainage problems or roof leaks.

Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask your local building department about drainage rules for your lot.

Storm preparation FAQ

When should I clean my gutters?

Late spring and late fall are common times, and after any big storm that drops leaves or branches.

Does a battery backup for a sump pump help?

Yes, because storms often cause power failures at the same time the pump is needed most.

Can I prevent all storm water damage?

No, but you can reduce the risk and limit the damage by acting fast.
