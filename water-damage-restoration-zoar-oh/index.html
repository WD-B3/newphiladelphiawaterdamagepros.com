Zoar is a village of about 170 people in northern Tuscarawas County, off I-77 and Route 212. It was founded in 1817 by German Separatists, and many of its buildings from the 1800s still stand. The village sits near the Tuscarawas River and the old Ohio and Erie Canal route. We reach Zoar from New Philadelphia in about eighteen minutes under normal conditions, and the line is open 24/7.

Old buildings dry differently

Most homes and shops in Zoar are not recent builds. Old timber frames, plaster, brick, stone and thick wood floors are common. These materials hold moisture longer than modern drywall and engineered lumber. A leak that a new house would shake off can stay in an old wall for weeks.

Structural drying is the heart of a Zoar job. Moisture mapping, steady airflow and dehumidifiers matter more here than fast demolition, since old plaster and original wood are worth saving where they can be dried. We explain the method on our structural drying and dehumidification page.

Many Zoar buildings are historic. We do not do preservation or restoration of historic fabric, and we do not claim any historic credentials. If a building has protected status, tell us when you call, and bring in the owner's preservation contact before anything is cut.

The river, the levee and flood risk

Zoar sits close to the Tuscarawas River, and its flat bottomland has been flooded before. The village is protected by a levee and a diversion dam that were built as part of the Muskingum Watershed flood control system after the great flood of 1913. Levees reduce risk, but they do not remove it, and local drainage, rain and groundwater still cause trouble.

For your parcel, look at the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask the county about floodplain rules. We do not quote flood zone status for any street.

If water is still spreading in Zoar: what to do before we arrive

Our emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Zoar. Because the village is a drive from New Philadelphia, a quick call gets a crew moving sooner. When the schedule allows, we respond the same day.

Close the valve

Shut the main or fixture valve if you can reach it safely. Old valves can stick, so do not force one that will not turn.

Power off for wet areas

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry. Older wiring needs extra care.

Protect what you can

Lift rugs, books and small furniture and take photos of the damage first.

Keep wet wood alone

Do not pry up old floors or trim. Wet wood can be dried in place in many cases.

Seasons in a small valley village

Winter cold finds old plumbing in stone cellars and unheated porches. Spring rain and thaw raise the water table and test the sump. Summer humidity slows drying. Fall storms bring wind to old roofs, which is when ceilings start to stain.

The ground in the Tuscarawas valley is not the same everywhere, and Zoar's low spots are different from the hillsides nearby. Photos, a clear description of the room and the age of the building help us plan equipment.

Getting to Zoar

Zoar is 2.9 road miles east of I-77 along Route 212. We come up I-77 north from New Philadelphia, which is roughly thirteen miles. Give the dispatcher the street name and a nearby landmark, such as the village green, the canal path or the Zoar Village State Memorial grounds, because house numbers can be hard to find on small village streets.
