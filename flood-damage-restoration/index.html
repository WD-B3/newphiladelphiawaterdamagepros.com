Floodwater in a house is an emergency from the first inch. This page covers flood damage cleanup in New Philadelphia, OH, meaning water that arrives from outside or rises from below. That includes river overflow, runoff after heavy rain and groundwater pushing through a basement wall. Our 24/7 line is answered at night, on weekends and on holidays.

While you wait for a crew, stay out of standing water if any outlet, cord or appliance is submerged. Cut power at the main panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

When floodwater is an emergency

Moving water and rising water both change the plan. A few inches of water that stopped spreading is a different job from a basement filling by the hour. Tell the dispatcher the depth, where it entered, whether the furnace or water heater is below the waterline and whether anyone in the house needs to leave.

Floodwater carries soil, runoff and sometimes sewage. Treat it as contaminated until a trained crew says otherwise. Do not run a household vacuum on it and do not walk through it barefoot.

Electrical and gas first

Water that touches an outlet, panel or extension cord can energize the whole wet area. If the panel is in a flooded basement, do not wade to it. Call the electric utility or 911 and tell them water is rising near the panel. Call our line after the hazard is handled.

What floods in this part of Ohio

New Philadelphia sits along the Tuscarawas River at about 899 feet above sea level, and the whole county has a long record with flooding. The Great Flood of March 1913 devastated Dover and the rest of Tuscarawas County, and it led to the flood control system run by the Muskingum Watershed Conservancy District. Dams reduce the risk. They do not remove it for every street and every basement.

Do not guess your own exposure from a neighbor. Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask the city building department what applies to your lot. Homes near the river, near low creek crossings or at the bottom of a slope tend to see water first, but a hard summer storm can overwhelm a storm drain on a high street too.

Signs the damage is bigger than it looks

  • Water line stains on drywall that sit well above the floor.
  • Baseboards pulling away from the wall or swollen trim.
  • A musty smell that returns after the surface dries.
  • Warped doors, sticking windows or a floor that feels soft.
  • Silt or a gritty film left on concrete and carpet.

How we handle a flood loss

Flood cleanup follows a sequence because each step protects the next one. Skipping ahead to fans before the water is out only moves moisture into the walls.

Stop and secure

Confirm the source has stopped, the power is safe and people and pets are out of the water.

Remove the water

Submersible pumps and truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water from floors and low spaces.

Remove what cannot dry

Saturated carpet pad, insulation and soaked drywall that touched floodwater are cut out and bagged.

Dry and verify

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while moisture meters track every wall and floor until readings reach dry standards.

The work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard, which you can ask any company about. For the drying stage in detail, see our structural drying and dehumidification page, and for the lowest room in the house, our basement water damage cleanup page.

Water categories matter

Floodwater is usually treated as Category 3 water, which means contaminated. That changes what can be saved.

Flood damage FAQ

Can you come out at night if river water gets into the house?

Yes. Flood calls are answered around the clock, including nights and holidays. Same-day response is offered when a crew is open.

Is floodwater always contaminated?

Treat it that way. Water that came from outside, from a river or from the ground can carry soil, runoff and bacteria. A trained crew classifies the water before deciding what to remove.

Should I start pumping the basement myself?

Not while power is on and not while the house is still taking on water. Pumping too fast can also stress a wall that the soil is still pushing on. Wait for guidance or let a crew set the pace.

How long does flood cleanup take in a typical home?

Extraction is often finished in a day. Drying commonly takes several days and depends on water depth, materials and humidity. Moisture readings decide when it is done, not the calendar.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Usually not. Flood is commonly excluded and covered by a separate flood policy, so check your policy or ask your insurer before you assume.
