How distance works for us

Distance matters in a water loss because every extra minute is another minute of damage. Dover is about four miles from our base, a short drive along the corridor shared with New Philadelphia. Zoar is about thirteen miles north, reached by Interstate 77 and State Route 212. Drive times are estimates and change with traffic, weather and road work.

If you are outside those three towns, call anyway. We look at where the crew is, where you are and how urgent the situation is, then tell you plainly what is possible. If we cannot reach you promptly, we say so, and we suggest what to do in the meantime.

What shapes water damage in each town

New Philadelphia and Dover share a river valley and an old housing stock, so basements, aging plumbing and heavy rain are the common themes. The Tuscarawas River has a long history of flooding in this valley, and the March 1913 flood is the event local flood control grew from.

Zoar is different. It is a small village with many buildings from the 1800s, built of timber, brick and stone. Old materials hold moisture and dry slowly, so drying matters more than demolition there. The levee that protects the village was built as part of the regional flood control system, but local drainage and roof leaks are still everyday risks.

When to call the line instead of reading

Most of this page is for planning. If you are standing in water, skip the reading and call. The dispatcher asks for your address first, so keep it ready, along with the nearest cross street. If you are in a rental, have the landlord's name handy as well.

Tell the dispatcher what you can see: where the water is coming from, how deep it is, which rooms it has reached and whether anything electrical is wet. Those few facts decide which equipment goes on the truck.
