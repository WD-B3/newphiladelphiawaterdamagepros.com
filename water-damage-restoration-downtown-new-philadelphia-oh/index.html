Downtown New Philadelphia is the street grid around Courthouse Square, with High Avenue and Broadway as the two main streets. The Tuscarawas County Courthouse on the square was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on July 16, 1973. The town was laid out in 1804 and incorporated in 1833, so the center has had a long time to collect old buildings and older plumbing. If water is moving through a downtown home, apartment or shop right now, call (740) 231-9132.

Houses, flats and shops share walls

Real estate descriptions of the city mention Victorian houses, apartment flats and single family homes. Downtown also has offices and storefronts, often with units above or beside them. We have not checked the age or pipe material of any particular downtown block, so treat the points below as general guidance. In a building with shared walls or floors, water from one unit can drip into the unit below or run along a wall into the next space.

That is why a stained ceiling in a downtown apartment deserves a quick look upstairs. Ask the neighbor above to check for a running toilet, a dripping tub or a washer hose, and tell your landlord the same day. The wet area keeps spreading until the source is found.

Drain work near the center

The city has spent money on storm drains close to downtown. In December 2022 the Board of Control was set to award the first phase of a storm sewer project meant to relieve flooding at Franklin Square on West High Avenue, with a low bid of $650,332. A later Fourth Street SW stormwater project, bid in April 2024, covered storm sewer, sanitary sewer and catch basin work at about $1.05 million in the southwest part of the city.

These projects show that the city is working on drainage. They do not mean every downtown basement floods. A cleared storm drain still cannot help a basement when a sink drain clogs or a supply line splits, and heavy rain can overload any system for a short time.

Frozen pipes and the shutoff that will not turn

In January 2025 the city's service director told local radio that the water department took 14 frozen-pipe calls in one week. The frozen pipes were inside homes or in crawl spaces, not in the service lines that enter the property. The same story described one downtown office where a pipe burst and an old shutoff valve could not be turned.

Learn where your main shutoff is before the cold arrives, and turn it once to see that it moves. If it will not budge, do not force it. A stuck valve is a plumber's job. When the pipe has already split, our work starts after the water is stopped, with extraction and drying.

What to do before we arrive

If water is spreading in a downtown building, these steps help most.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff only if you can reach it safely and it turns easily.

Warn the neighbors

In a shared building, tell the unit below and your landlord so they can check for leaks and move belongings.

Skip the breaker panel in water

Do not touch breakers or plugged in devices while standing in water. Call the electric utility if the panel is wet.

Photograph first

Take pictures of the damage and the source before anything is moved, when it is safe to do so.

Call the line

Call (740) 231-9132 with your street, the floor and what the water touched.

How a downtown job goes

Call

A dispatcher takes your address, the floor and what you see.

Assess

The crew checks the source, the water category and the moisture in walls and floors.

Remove

Standing water is extracted and wet material that cannot dry is taken out.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run with daily moisture readings until the structure is dry.

Our drying plan uses the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration as its guide. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help document the loss for your insurer. Whether your policy pays depends on the cause of the damage and the terms of the policy, so ask your agent how it reads yours. We do not decide coverage.
