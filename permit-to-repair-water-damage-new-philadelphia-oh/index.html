Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in New Philadelphia, OH?

Drying a wet house usually does not need a permit. Rebuilding it sometimes does, and the line depends on what you are replacing.

Water damage work happens in two stages. First comes mitigation: extraction, removal of wet material and drying. Then comes rebuild: new drywall, flooring, trim and paint. Permits, when they apply, mostly attach to the rebuild.

This article is general information and not legal advice. Rules vary by jurisdiction, and the only authority is your local building department.

Drying and extraction

Extracting water, setting air movers and dehumidifiers and removing wet carpet or damaged drywall are cleanup steps. They generally do not alter the structure or the building systems, and permits are not usually required for them. If a structure has asbestos or lead paint concerns, other rules can apply, and that is why testing matters in older buildings.

Contractors may still ask for documentation or permission from you, the owner, and in rental properties, from the landlord. Tell the crew if the building is older or has unusual materials.

Rebuild work

Rebuild work can need permits depending on the scope. Replacing drywall and painting is usually treated as repair. Replacing structural framing, modifying walls or changing layouts is more serious. Electrical and plumbing work often requires permits and licensed trades.

Floodplain rules

If your home is in a mapped floodplain, local floodplain rules may apply to repairs after a major loss. In communities that participate in the National Flood Insurance Program, there are rules for substantially damaged buildings, which are those where repair costs exceed a share of the building's value. The local floodplain administrator decides.

If you think this applies, ask the floodplain administrator before you rebuild. Our flood damage cleanup page describes the cleanup side.

Who issues permits

In Ohio, building codes are adopted at the state level, and local building departments enforce them. Depending on where your address is, the permit authority could be a city or the county. Your address decides, not your mailing city, so call before you assume.

Ask the local office what it needs, whether homeowner permits are allowed and what inspections it requires. Keep the permit documents with your insurance records.

Licensed trades

Electrical and plumbing work usually needs a licensed contractor. The state licenses plumbing and electrical contractors through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, and a license number can be checked. Ask any contractor you hire for a license number and verify it.

Questions to ask the building department

A phone call to the building department can settle most questions in a few minutes. Ask whether a permit is required for the specific work you plan, such as replacing a section of subfloor, rewiring a wet outlet circuit or replacing a damaged water line.

Ask what documents they need, whether a homeowner can pull the permit or whether a licensed contractor must, and what inspections follow. Write down the name of the person you spoke with and the date.

If you rent, the landlord is usually responsible for permits. If a contractor says no permit is needed, ask them to put that statement in the written scope. A reputable contractor will not mind.

Why permits matter even when they are annoying

A permit creates an inspection, and an inspection is a second pair of eyes on work that will be hidden behind drywall. Wiring that was wet and then reused, a plumbing joint that was patched in a hurry and framing that was cut and not replaced are the kinds of problems inspections catch.

Permits also leave a record. When you sell, a buyer's inspector or lender may ask whether repairs were permitted, and an unpermitted rebuild can slow a sale or require reinspection. If your insurer is paying for the rebuild, ask whether it expects permits and inspections as part of the claim.

If a contractor offers to skip a permit to save you money, treat that as a warning.

Building rules in Ohio

Ohio adopts statewide building codes through the Ohio Board of Building Standards in the Department of Commerce, and certified local building departments enforce them. Which office handles your address depends on whether it falls under a city or county department.

Tuscarawas County includes cities, villages and townships, so the right office can differ between New Philadelphia, Dover and Zoar. Ask the office that serves your parcel.

Permit FAQ

Does water damage cleanup need a permit?

Usually not. Extraction and drying are generally not permitted work, but rebuild work can be.

Who do I call about a permit in New Philadelphia?

Your local building department. Which one depends on your address, so confirm before you start.

Do I need a permit to replace drywall?

Often not for patches. Large scale replacement and wiring or plumbing changes may need one.

Does the permit rule differ in Dover and Zoar?

It can. The office depends on the address. See our Dover and Zoar pages for the service area.
