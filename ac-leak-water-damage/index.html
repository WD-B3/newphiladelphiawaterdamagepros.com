Air conditioners make water. Every cooling system pulls moisture from the air and drains it away through a condensate line. When that line clogs, freezes or cracks, the water goes into the ceiling, the attic or the wall.

AC leak water damage repair in New Philadelphia, OH covers what that leak wets and how it is dried. Our line is open 24/7, though many AC leaks are not emergencies if you can stop the system.

Turn off the air conditioner at the thermostat and the breaker, put a bucket under any drip and call. If water is dripping through a light fixture, stay away and shut off power to that circuit if it is safe.

What causes AC leaks

  • A clogged condensate drain line.
  • A cracked or disconnected drain pan.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that melts and overflows.
  • A failed condensate pump.
  • Poor drain slope or a loose joint.

Algae and dirt are the usual cause of a clog. A frozen coil is often a sign of low airflow, such as a dirty filter. An HVAC technician fixes the equipment. We deal with the wet materials.

Where the water goes

In an attic, water runs down insulation and into the ceiling drywall below. In a closet or basement, it spreads along the floor. Over a long period it can stain a ceiling in a ring, swell drywall seams and soak insulation until it clumps and loses its value.

Because the water is usually clean supply-quality condensate, much of it can be dried in place. Insulation that stayed wet is often replaced. Sagging or stained drywall is judged by moisture readings. Our ceiling water damage repair page covers the ceiling side in more depth.

Signs of an AC leak

  • A brown or yellow ring on a ceiling below an air handler.
  • Water in the secondary drain pan.
  • A musty smell near the return or in a closet.
  • Ice on the indoor unit or the refrigerant line.
  • Wet insulation or damp carpet near the air handler.

What we do

Stop the system

The HVAC system is off until the drain issue is fixed.

Map the moisture

Meters and thermal imaging show the wet ceiling, insulation and framing.

Remove what cannot dry

Wet insulation and failed drywall are cut out.

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers work the ceiling and attic cavities until readings return to normal.

After drying, any repair or repainting is a separate job. For the process, read our structural drying and dehumidification page, and for walls, our wall and drywall water damage repair page.

Preventing the next AC leak

Change the filter

A clean filter reduces the chance of a frozen coil.

Flush the drain line

A yearly flush helps keep algae from clogging the line.

Add a float switch

A switch in the drain pan shuts the system off before the pan overflows.

Look at the pan

A glance at the secondary pan during the cooling season can catch a slow drip.

AC leak FAQ

Is an AC leak an emergency?

Not always, but water coming through a ceiling or fixture is. Turn the system off and call. The line is open 24/7.

Do you fix the air conditioner?

No. An HVAC technician fixes the equipment. We dry the wet materials and document the loss.

Why is there water around my indoor unit?

A clogged condensate line, a cracked pan or a frozen coil are the usual reasons.

Can a leak cause mold?

Wet materials left for days can support mold growth. Prompt drying lowers the risk, and health questions belong to a physician.

Will insurance cover the damage?

It depends on whether the leak was sudden or long-term. A quick call to your agent tells you how your policy treats this kind of loss.
