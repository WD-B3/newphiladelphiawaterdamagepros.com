How to Shut Off Your Home's Water Main in New Philadelphia, OH

When a pipe bursts, you have minutes, not hours. Knowing where your main shutoff is and how it works is the most useful thing you can learn before an emergency.

By the New Philadelphia Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026.

A burst pipe can release gallons of water every minute. The fastest way to limit the damage is to close the main shutoff valve. The time to find it is before you need it.

Find yours today, label it and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is. Try the valve once a year to make sure it still turns.

Where to look

The main shutoff is where the water line enters the house. In homes with basements, look near the front foundation wall, close to where the meter is. In homes with crawl spaces or slabs, it may be in a utility closet, a garage, near the water heater or in a mechanical room.

Look for a pipe coming out of the floor or wall with a valve on it and, usually, a water meter nearby. The valve is either a round wheel handle or a lever.

Two valve types

ValveHow it looksHow to close it
Gate valveRound wheel handleTurn clockwise until it stops.
Ball valveLever handleTurn a quarter turn so the handle is crosswise to the pipe.

Gate valves can stick or break when forced. Turn gently. If a gate valve will not turn, do not use a wrench with great force. Call a plumber to replace it.

The street valve

Most properties also have a curb stop, a valve near the street or in the yard, usually in a small cover marked water. The utility controls this valve, and you generally should not operate it yourself. If your inside valve fails, call the utility or an emergency plumber.

After you close the main

Open a faucet

Open the lowest faucet to drain remaining pressure.

Turn off the water heater

Switch off the power or gas so the tank does not run dry and overheat.

Document

Photograph the leak and the water.

Call

Call a plumber for the repair and us for water removal.

If water is near electricity, stay out and call the utility or 911. Do not stand in water to reach a panel.

Fixture shutoffs

Toilets, sinks and washing machines have their own small shutoff valves. Turn them clockwise to close. For an overflowing toilet, close the valve behind it or lift the tank lid and press the flapper down. For a washing machine, close both the hot and cold valves behind it.

Test and label

Once a year, close and reopen the main to make sure it works, and tag it with a bright label. Take a photo of the valve and keep it with your insurance records. If it is hard to reach, clear the area around it.

If you have a well or a different water system, the layout differs. Ask a plumber to show you the shutoffs in your home.

What happens next

Closing the main stops the leak, not the damage. Water already in the house keeps soaking into materials. See our water extraction page for the next step, and our burst pipe water damage page for the full picture. For winter, see frozen pipe water damage.

Practice before you need it

Once you have found the valve, practice. Close it fully, open a faucet at the lowest level of the house to see that the flow stops, then open the valve again. If the valve is stiff, spray it with penetrating oil and work it slowly back and forth over a few days.

Tell everyone who lives in the home where the valve is. Children old enough to help can be shown, and a house sitter or a visitor should know. If you have a rental, tell the tenant, and if you are a tenant, ask the landlord.

Keep a flashlight near the valve, and clear boxes and clutter from around it. In an emergency, you will not want to move storage bins in the dark.

If your shutoff is broken or missing

Some older homes have a main valve that no longer seals, or no accessible main at all. A plumber can replace a failing gate valve with a ball valve, which is more reliable, and can add a shutoff where one is missing. It is usually a short job and a good investment before the first cold snap.

Plumbing standards such as the Ohio plumbing code require accessible shutoff valves in many installations, and your plumber can tell you what applies to your home.

Winter and the main valve

Northeast Ohio winters bring hard freezes, and pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls are the usual casualties. When a pipe splits in winter, the water often keeps running after the thaw begins, so the main valve matters most in the hours after a cold snap breaks.

Know that the utility that serves your address owns the meter and the street valve. Call it if the problem is outside your property.

Water main FAQ

What if I cannot find my main shutoff?

Look at the meter, which is usually near it. If you still cannot find it, call the utility or a plumber and ask them to show you.

What if the valve will not turn?

Do not force it. Call a plumber to replace it, and use fixture shutoffs in the meantime.

Should I turn off the water when I travel?

In winter or for long trips, many people do. It reduces the chance of a leak running unnoticed.
