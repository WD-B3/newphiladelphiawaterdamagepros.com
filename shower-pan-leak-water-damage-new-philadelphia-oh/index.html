Shower Pan Leak Water Damage in New Philadelphia, OH

A shower pan leak can run for months before anyone notices. The water goes into the subfloor and ceiling below, and the signs are easy to miss.

By the New Philadelphia Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026.

A shower pan is the waterproof base under a shower. When it fails, water escapes under the tile and into the floor system. The shower looks fine, but the damage keeps growing below it.

This article explains the signs, how the water spreads, who repairs what and what to expect from drying. It is general information, and a plumber or tile contractor will diagnose the pan itself.

Signs of a shower pan leak

  • A stain or bubbling paint on the ceiling below the bathroom.
  • A musty smell near the shower that stays after cleaning.
  • Loose, cracked or hollow-sounding tile on the shower floor.
  • Water collecting at the base of the shower after use.
  • Soft or spongy flooring around the shower.
  • Swollen baseboards or a bulging wall outside the shower.

Any one of these can have other causes. Several together point strongly at a leak.

Why the damage is hard to see

Most pans sit above the subfloor, with tile on top. Water that gets past the liner or the drain flange soaks the mortar bed, the subfloor and the joists. It can then travel along framing before it shows on a ceiling or a wall, so the stain is rarely right under the leak.

Because the water is clean at first, damage builds slowly. Over time, wood softens, fasteners rust and mold can start. A leak found early can cost far less to fix than one found after the subfloor has failed.

How leaks are confirmed

A plumber can test a shower pan by plugging the drain and filling the pan with water to see if the level drops. This should be done by someone who understands the test, since it can push water into the floor if done wrong. A moisture meter on the ceiling below and on the surrounding floor shows how far the water has gone.

Do not guess. Cracked grout or a failed caulk joint can leak water too, but those are different repairs from a failed pan liner or drain.

Who fixes what

Plumber or tile contractor

  • Finds the leak in the pan or drain.
  • Replaces the liner, drain or pan.
  • Retiles and regrouts the shower.

Water damage restoration

  • Extracts water and maps moisture.
  • Dries the subfloor, joists and walls.
  • Removes materials that cannot dry.

The pan has to be repaired or the water will return. Drying has to be done or the structure stays wet. Both are needed, and the order depends on the damage.

What drying involves

Stop the water

Stop using the shower until it is tested and repaired.

Map moisture

Meters and thermal imaging show wet areas.

Open and dry

Wet drywall or flooring is opened and equipment is placed.

Verify

Readings confirm the structure is dry before rebuild.

Drywall damage below is covered on our wall and drywall water damage repair page, and ceilings on our ceiling water damage repair page. The drying process is explained on our structural drying page.

What it costs

A shower pan leak costs depend on how long it went on and what is wet. A caught-early leak may need a pan repair and a few days of drying. A late leak can mean replacing subfloor and framing. You hear the scope and price before any work begins.

Insurance sometimes covers the sudden part of a leak and excludes long-term seepage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

What to do while you wait for repairs

Stop using the shower. Every shower sends several gallons into a failed pan, and that water goes into the floor system. Use another bathroom if you have one, and keep the room ventilated with a fan or an open window to help the air dry.

If a ceiling below is stained or bulging, keep people out from under it and place a bucket if it drips. Photograph the stain and the shower, and note the date. Photos taken before repairs help show the extent of the damage.

Do not caulk the shower and assume it is fixed. Caulk helps with joint leaks, but a failed pan liner or drain flange needs a proper repair, and sealing the surface can trap moisture below.

Preventing the next one

Most shower pan leaks start where water is not supposed to be: a pinhole in the liner, a loose drain flange or a corner that was folded badly. Good installation matters more than any product, so ask who flood tests the pan after installation.

Keep grout and caulk in good shape, and reseal natural stone and grout on the schedule the manufacturer gives. Fix small cracks early.

Drying after a shower pan leak follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and the plumbing repair follows the Ohio plumbing code your plumber works under.

Plumbing licensing in Ohio

Plumbers working in Ohio are licensed through the state's Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, so you can ask a plumber for their license number and verify it. That is a practical check before hiring someone to diagnose or replace a shower pan.

Many homes in the New Philadelphia area were built decades ago, and bathrooms in older houses may have original plumbing. If your shower is older than the house's last remodel, tell the contractor.

Shower pan leak FAQ

How do I know if my shower pan is leaking?

A stain on the ceiling below, loose tile, a musty smell or soft flooring are common signs. A plumber can test the pan.

Can I keep using the shower?

It is better not to, since every use puts water in the floor.

Can a leaking shower cause mold?

It can, if the area stays damp. Drying and fixing the leak reduce that risk.
