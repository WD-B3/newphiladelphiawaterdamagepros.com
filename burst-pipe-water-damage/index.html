A burst pipe is a pressurized leak, and it can put a lot of water through a house in a short time. Emergency burst pipe water damage cleanup in New Philadelphia, OH starts with the shutoff and ends with a dry, verified structure. The line is open 24/7 because pipes do not fail on business hours.

Shut off the main water valve now if you can find it. Then open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, turn off power to wet areas only if it is safe, lift valuables off the floor and call.

What to do in the first ten minutes

Shut off the main

Turn the main valve clockwise until it stops. It is often near the water meter or where the line enters the house.

Cut the heater

If the water heater is affected, turn off its power or gas supply.

Protect people and power

Keep everyone out of wet rooms with active outlets or a ceiling that is sagging.

Photograph and call

Take quick photos of the pipe and wet areas, then call the 24/7 line.

If you are not sure where your main valve is, the dispatcher can help you work through the likely places while you are still on the line.

When a burst pipe is an emergency

Every burst pipe is urgent, but some are worse. A break inside a wall sprays into insulation and drywall and can run for a long time before it shows. A break above a finished ceiling can bring the ceiling down.

A break in a cold unheated space may have more than one split. Tell the dispatcher where you think the water is coming from and whether it has stopped.

Ceilings and electricity

A ceiling that is bulging with water can fall without warning. Stay out from under it. If water is running through light fixtures or near the panel, stay out of the room and call the electric utility or 911 before you call us.

Why pipes burst in this area

Supply lines fail for a few common reasons. Freezing is one, and it is covered on our frozen pipe water damage page. Corrosion at joints, high water pressure, a failed fitting or a damaged line during other work cause the rest. Older galvanized and some early copper systems are more prone to pinhole failures that grow.

Ohio winters are cold enough to threaten exposed lines in garages, outside walls and crawl spaces, so a hard freeze followed by a thaw is a common trigger. Ask your water utility about your service line pressure if you suspect it runs high.

What the damage looks like

  • Water stains that spread across a ceiling or down a wall.
  • Bubbling or peeling paint and soft drywall.
  • Warped baseboards and flooring.
  • Sound of running water when everything is off.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure.

How the repair and drying are handled

A plumber fixes the pipe. We handle the water and what it soaked. Those are different trades, and a restoration crew does not replace your plumbing.

After the source is fixed, crews extract standing water, cut out any wet insulation or drywall that cannot dry, and set air movers and dehumidifiers. Moisture meters guide the work until the framing and subfloor reach dry readings.

Burst pipes in kitchens often wet cabinets, so see kitchen water damage restoration for that side. Walls are covered on our wall and drywall water damage repair page.

Burst pipe FAQ

What if the pipe breaks in the middle of the night?

Close the main, then call. The line is open all night, and a dispatcher walks you through safe steps while a crew is arranged.

Do you fix the pipe?

No. A licensed plumber repairs the pipe. We extract the water, dry the structure and document the loss. We can work alongside your plumber.

Where is my main shutoff valve?

Usually near the water meter, in a basement or utility room, or where the service line enters the house. Find it before you need it and label it.

How soon should drying start?

As soon as the source is stopped and standing water is out. Wet drywall and insulation can start to smell within a couple of days.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for a burst pipe?

Sudden and accidental breaks are often covered. Check your policy or ask your insurer for the specifics.
