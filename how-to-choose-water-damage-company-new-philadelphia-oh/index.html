How to Choose a Water Damage Company in New Philadelphia, OH

You will probably choose a water damage company in a hurry, at a bad time. A short list of questions helps you pick well even then.

By the New Philadelphia Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026.
24/7 Emergency Line.
(740) 231-9132
Call Now

Water damage companies advertise loudly, and not all of them work the same way. The difference shows up in how they answer a handful of questions. Ask them before you sign anything.

This guide is written by a water damage company, so read it with that in mind. The questions apply to us, too.

Do they answer and show up

Water damage does not wait. A company that answers the phone at all hours and tells you honestly when it can arrive is a good start. Be cautious of anyone who guarantees an exact arrival time without knowing where you are or how bad the water is.

We offer same-day response when available, and we say so plainly when it is not.

What will they do and how will they prove it

Ask what the process is, in plain words. A good answer includes extraction, removal of materials that cannot dry, drying with equipment and moisture readings to confirm the result. Ask whether they follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. It is a standard, not a license, so ask how they use it.

Ask to see moisture readings during and at the end of the job. If the company cannot show numbers, it cannot show the job is done.

Red flags

  • Pressure to sign immediately or a contract that hands over your claim rights.
  • A price given over the phone with no inspection.
  • No mention of moisture readings or drying equipment.
  • Demolition of everything with no explanation.
  • Promises about what your insurer will pay.
  • No written scope.

A company should explain why something is being removed. If a wall reads dry, there is no reason to cut it out.

Beware of direct billing promises

Some companies ask you to assign your insurance claim to them. Read any such document carefully, since it can give them control over the claim. You may sign with whoever you like, and you have the right to ask questions first.

Scope and price

A good scope lists what will be extracted, what will be removed, what equipment will be used and for how many days, and what is not included. The price should match. Ask what changes the price, such as extra drying days or hidden damage found later.

Reading a scope of work

A scope is the document that says what a company will do. A good one lists rooms, materials to remove, equipment to place, the number of drying days it expects and what is not included. It should mention that the plan may change if moisture readings show more or less damage than expected.

Compare scopes line by line, not only by total. If one company plans to remove all drywall to a certain height and another plans to dry in place, ask each to explain. The difference may be a different reading of the damage.

Ask what happens if drying takes longer. Some companies bill by the day for equipment, and others quote a fixed price. Either can be fair if it is explained in writing before work begins.

Check references and local presence

Ask for two or three recent local customers you can contact, or for before and after photos of similar jobs. Check that the address and phone number the company gives you are real and local, and that the crew that shows up carries the same name as the company you called.

A company that is easy to reach after the job is done is more likely to stand behind it. Ask what happens if a reading rises again after equipment leaves.

Verify licenses in Ohio

For plumbing, electrical, HVAC and similar trades the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board is the state licensing body, so ask for a license number whenever the work requires one. Water damage mitigation itself is not covered by one statewide license, so ask what applies to each part of your job.

Check reviews from real customers on sites you trust, and ask for local references.

Choosing a company FAQ

Should I get several quotes?

If time allows, yes. In an active flood, stop the water first and call. A company can start extraction while you compare details.

Is the cheapest company the best?

Not necessarily. A low price with no moisture monitoring can cost more later.

Do I have to use my insurer's preferred company?

Generally you may choose your own contractor. Ask your insurer about your policy.
