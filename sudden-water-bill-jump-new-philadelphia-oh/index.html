Sudden Water Bill Jump in New Philadelphia, OH: Is It a Leak?

A water bill that doubles with no change in habits is a clue. Here is how to check for a leak before the damage shows.

By the New Philadelphia Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026.
24/7 Emergency Line.
(740) 231-9132
Call Now

A sudden jump in your water bill can mean a hidden leak. Leaks in toilets, irrigation lines, supply pipes and under slabs can run day and night without a visible puddle. The sooner you find one, the less damage it does.

Higher bills have other causes too, like a bill with an estimated read, a season of lawn watering or guests staying with you. Check the bill and run the tests below.

Read the bill first

Look at the usage line, not just the total. Compare gallons or cubic feet with the same period last year, and note whether the read was actual or estimated. A one time spike can be a catch up after an estimated read.

A bill that rises and stays high points to a continuing leak. Rates and fees can also change, so compare usage, not only dollars.

Do a meter test

Turn off all water inside and outside the house. Make sure no appliance is running, including the ice maker and the water softener if you have one. Read the meter, wait 30 minutes without using water, and read it again.

If the meter moved, water is going somewhere. If the meter has a small triangle or dial that spins when no water is used, that is a leak indicator on many meters. Note the number and call your utility if you need help reading it.

Toilet leaks are the most common cause of hidden water waste. A silent leak from the tank to the bowl can add thousands of gallons a month.

When the leak is under the house

If the meter moves and you cannot find a source inside or outside, the leak may be in a pipe in the wall or under a slab. Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water and cracked tile can be signs. Our slab leak water damage page explains the drying side, and a plumber finds and repairs the leak.

Also check basements and crawl spaces for damp spots, mineral stains and mold. If you find water damage, our water extraction page describes what we do.

What to do once you find it

Shut off the water

Close the main or the fixture valve to stop the leak.

Call a plumber

A licensed plumber repairs the pipe or fixture.

Dry the area

Wet materials need drying to avoid damage and mold.

Ask the utility

Some utilities offer a leak adjustment on the bill. Ask.

Insurance and the bill

Insurance often pays for damage caused by a sudden pipe break but may not cover the cost of the water that leaked, or the cost to find the leak. Policies differ. Your policy and your insurer decide coverage, so read the water damage section or call your agent.

Leaks that do not show up on a meter test

Some leaks only run under pressure at certain times. A water heater relief valve can drip only when the tank heats. A sprinkler or irrigation valve can leak only when a zone runs. A slow dripping faucet adds up but may not move the meter in thirty minutes.

If the meter test is clean but the bill is still high, compare a few weeks of daily readings. Write down the meter number each morning and evening. Patterns that show use at night, when nobody is awake, point to a leak. A higher bill during a month with guests, a pool fill or lawn watering has a simpler explanation.

Also check for a stuck water softener regeneration cycle, a humidifier on your furnace and an outdoor spigot left open.

Ask the utility for help

Utilities see a lot of leaks and can often tell you when use rose and whether it looks steady. Ask for a usage history and whether the meter was recently changed or the read was estimated. Some utilities will inspect the meter if you request it.

Keep copies of the repair invoice. If the utility offers a leak adjustment, a repair receipt is usually what it asks for.

Plumbing guidelines used by licensed plumbers treat a toilet leak and a supply line leak as separate repairs, and the meter test above separates them for you.

Water service and winter in Tuscarawas County

The utility on your bill is the one to call about the meter, and the account shows whether your address is served by a city system or another provider. Ask whether it offers a leak adjustment.

Northeast Ohio winters bring hard freezes, which can split outdoor spigot lines and pipes in unheated spaces. A frozen and split line can run for days after a thaw, so check your bill after a cold snap.

Water bill FAQ

How much of a bill jump suggests a leak?

A rise of more than a small amount with no change in habits is worth testing. A meter test settles it.

Can a leak under a slab raise my bill?

Yes. Slab leaks run constantly and can raise a bill without any visible water.

Does a restoration company find leaks?

We find wet materials with meters and thermal imaging. A plumber finds and repairs the leak.
